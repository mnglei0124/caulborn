# Main Cast — Personalities & Arcs

Slots are noted against Undertale's archetypes for design shorthand only — every character must diverge from their slot in at least one load-bearing way, noted as **Divergence**.

## The player — "Lark" (default name; player can rename)
Silent vessel. A child who fell through a wound in the Caul. No thread, casts anyway. Default name "Lark": a bird that sings at dawn, in a world with no dawn — and also "a lark," a bit of fun. NPCs who know old-world words react to the name strangely.
- The game never voices Lark. Choices are actions. NPCs project onto them constantly, and the projections characterize the *NPCs*.
- **Rename hook:** the Guest is the only character who refuses to use the player's chosen name. What the Guest calls them instead (later arcs) is a lore reveal.

## Matron Bell — Hush master, keeper of the Vigil hospice *(Toriel-slot, Ch.1 boss)*
Elderly, dry-witted, moves like a glacier with opinions. Runs the Vigil, the Fallows hospice where the Thin spend their last Vigils. Rings a hand-bell at each snuffing; she has rung it more times than anyone should. Took one look at a child who fell out of the sky and decided, instantly and without discussion, that the child lives with her now.
- **Warmth:** teaches the player the Fallows, feeds them, jokes with the dying like they're regulars at her pub.
- **Wound:** she has spent decades casting Hush to anchor her patients a little longer. She is going Thin herself — her left hand is already translucent, and she wears a glove. Everyone knows. No one says.
- **Boss fight:** she will not let the child leave for Wicktown, where the Cantors are already asking questions. Her fight is a lullaby — dense slow Hush lattices that *herd you back toward the door of the Vigil*, and if you simply stop dodging, the fight gently ends with you tucked into bed (a fail-state that's also characterization). Mercy solution: endure and keep walking forward — show her you can survive being loved by something that can't keep you.
- **Arc:** learning to let someone leave. On mercy routes she becomes the campaign's hearth — and her Thinning progresses, which the true ending must confront (rule: nothing heals Thin). On the violence route hers is the first ash the game offers. The bell is left ringing.
- **Divergence from slot:** Toriel's fight tests your resolve to hurt her; Bell's tests your resolve to *keep moving while she begs you, kindly, to stop existing at risk*. Also: she does not step aside at the end — you must Still her, and she walks you to the gate herself.

## Fenn — Threadless kid, self-declared "wizard" *(Papyrus-slot, deuteragonist)*
Twelve-ish, loud, luminous, Threadless — and absolutely refuses to accept it. Builds "spells" from junk: bottle rockets, marbles, mirrors, an extremely patient dog. His bullet patterns are literally contraptions wheeled into the fight. Follows the player out of the Fallows because a threadless kid who casts is *proof it's possible*.
- **Warmth:** the funniest character in the game and the most sincere. His encounter is unloseable-ish and mostly involves his machines misfiring in escalating, elaborate ways.
- **Wound:** "dim." He's been called it his whole life. His bravado is armor over the belief that he is a hole in the world.
- **Arc (mercy):** discovers what Threadless means — the only hands that can touch the Caul. He doesn't get magic; he gets *purpose*, and it's better. Endgame: Fenn mends the wound the player fell through — or holds it open. His choice, shaped by how the player treated him.
- **Arc (violence):** he stands in the road anyway. He's the one fight where the game gives you every possible off-ramp, and if you take none — that's the point of no return, the mark the rest of the route wears.
- **Divergence:** Papyrus wants to capture you to be someone; Fenn wants to BE you. His arc is about un-learning envy of the protagonist.

## Cantrix Veil — Fervor inquisitor of the Mendery *(Undyne-slot, recurring hunter)*
A Cantor — sings her spells; her boss fights are rhythm-synced bullet patterns (the music IS the attack telegraphing). Sent to find the outsider, because an outsider means a wound, and a wound means the end of everything she has sworn to hold shut.
- **Warmth:** righteous, not cruel. Off-duty she's awkward, over-literal, devastating at card games, tone-deaf in speech and perfect in song.
- **Wound:** she is *correct*. There IS a wound. The player IS proof. The mid-game reveal (Layer 2: the quarantine) breaks her faith's frame entirely — the fight where she learns it, her hymn collapses mid-battle and her Fervor patterns crack into raw, unsynced Rue. That fight is the tonal center of the game.
- **Arc:** faith → evidence → chosen faith. On mercy routes she becomes the player's sword-arm and the Mendery's reformer. Her true-ending role: sings the Ward's found name — the only voice trained to carry it.
- **Divergence:** Undyne hunts you for duty and becomes your friend through comedy; Veil hunts you because she's *right*, and befriending her requires making her wrong about the world, which nearly destroys her.

## Curator Sallow — Rue master, keeper of the Archive *(Alphys-slot inverted)*
Half-Thin, entirely cheerful about it. Keeps their own memories in labeled jars (Rue-craft) and consults them mid-conversation: "Hold on. I knew this yesterday. Let me check." Dry, morbid, delighted by the player like a birder spotting an extinct species.
- Keeper of the Layer 2 truth. Technically illegal; kept alive because the crown needs a memory the church can't edit.
- **Comedy → grief pipeline:** the same forgetting played for laughs in Chapter 3 lands, once, quietly, on something that matters — they forget the player's name at a crucial moment, mid-sentence, and we watch them realize the jar for it is empty.
- **Arc:** a person deciding what to spend their last memories ON. They know the truth of the world and are rationing themselves to live long enough to tell the right person.
- **Divergence:** Alphys lies from shame; Sallow tells the truth relentlessly and forgets it. Their crisis isn't confession — it's succession.

## The Lamplighter — celebrity, monster with a marketing team *(Mettaton-slot, Ch.2 & 5 antagonist)*
Beloved civic hero of Wicktown: brightens districts, kisses babies, hosts the bright-festivals. Powered — state-sanctioned, quietly — by consuming ash of the Thin who slip away in civic hospices. "They weren't using it anymore." The game's argument that consumption can smile.
- Fights are spectacle: Vim/Fervor hybrid, stage machinery, crowd-pleaser patterns that are lethal *and* the crowd loves them. On the violence route, the Lamplighter doesn't fight the player — he offers a partnership. The route's darkest mirror is that he means it.
- **Arc:** exposure or inheritance. Mercy route exposes the Charter; violence route the player *becomes* the supply chain.
- **Divergence:** Mettaton wants ratings; the Lamplighter wants to be *necessary* — he genuinely keeps morale alive in a dying world and genuinely eats the dead to do it. He is the Refuge Myth as a person.

## Warden Ossian — the king on the guttering throne *(Asgore-slot)*
Sixty Vigils on the throne, mostly Thin, anchor of the Ward's dream. The palace rations his lucid hours; the Lumen is governed by his stewards in his name. In his rare clear moments he is gentle, courteous, funny in a way that predates everyone alive — and he knows exactly what he is: a man being slowly eaten by the world's survival, who signed up for it.
- **Endgame:** descending to the Ward requires unthreading him — the master-thread runs through the throne. The final approach is a conversation with a man flickering between three ages of himself. His "fight" is the Ward's raw dream pouring through what's left of him: the only encounter using all five schools' grammars at once, phases keyed to which self is surfacing.
- **Divergence:** Asgore is a coward with a plan he hates; Ossian is brave with a duty that worked — the system isn't broken, it's *functioning as designed*, and that's the indictment.

## The Guest — the previous faller *(Flowey/Chara-slot, candle companion)*
Voice at the save-candles. Warm, funny, knows too much, never blinks at your deaths — comments on them, in fact, before you've told anyone. Centuries ago they fell as the player fell; they reached the Ward and were kept: folded into the dream, aware, unable to die.
- **Function:** tutorializes the loop diegetically; Wit master; unreliable narrator of every layer of history (they were THERE for none of it but have read the dream's records — secondhand omniscience with an agenda).
- **Agenda:** out. The player is the first door in centuries. Every kindness the Guest shows is real AND instrumental — both at once, always.
- **Endgame branches:** true ending must decide what the player owes them (they are, structurally, the player's dark twin — what falling in *usually* costs). Violence route: the Guest is the final voice, and what they do with a Weight-heavy player is the route's last horror. NOT a boss in the conventional sense on any route — the Guest cannot gutter. Fighting them is a category error the game refuses to let you make.
- **Divergence:** Flowey is what a player becomes without consequences; the Guest is what a player becomes *without a body* — pure spectator hunger. They don't want to toy with the world. They want hands again.

## Recurring texture cast (sketches — expand per chapter)
- **The Vigil residents** (Ch.1): three named Thin patients whose stories seed later chapters' lore. One of them knew the Guest's era.
- **Grower Ossa** (Ch.4, Vim master): sketch only. Tends the flesh-gardens that feed the Lumen; their secret is the gardens' seed-stock origin. Name collision with Ossian is intentional — old families.
- **The dog:** Fenn's dog. Appears in patterns. Unkillable. Non-negotiable.

## Relationship web (track in CONTINUITY.md as it grows)
Bell ↔ Sallow: old friends; Sallow jars a memory for her each Vigil. Veil ↔ Lamplighter: public allies, private contempt. Fenn ↔ Bell: she raised him after the Fallows shrugged. Ossian ↔ the Guest: Ossian's grandmother wrote the sealed report on the "second faller." Every pair above has at least one scene per arc where the player's route changes its temperature.
