# World & Lore Bible — The Lumen

## The shape of the world
The known world is the **Lumen**: a landlocked country of roughly seven regions, sealed on every side — above, below, around — by a living membrane called the **Caul**. There is no sun, no stars, no horizon. The Caul glows faintly and unevenly, giving the Lumen a perpetual violet-amber twilight. Time is kept in **brights** and **dims** (the Caul's slow pulse, roughly a day-cycle) and counted in **Vigils** (years).

Anatomically, a *lumen* is the space enclosed by a membrane. Scholars inside the world know this and find it funny in a way that stops being funny if you think about it too long. A *caul* is the membrane some children are born wearing over their faces — folklore says caul-born children cannot drown and see what others can't. Everyone in this world is caul-born. The folklore is the state religion.

## The three layers of history
The truth about the Lumen is revealed in layers across the arcs. **Each layer is true as far as it goes.** This is the spine of the entire story — everything else hangs on it.

### Layer 1 — The Refuge Myth (what everyone believes)
Long ago the outside world burned in a catastrophe called the **Sundering**. The Founders wove the Caul to shelter the survivors, and sealed inside with them the last living fragment of the old world's heart — the **Ward** — whose dreaming sustains the Caul and whose overflowing vitality is the source of all magic. The Wardens (the royal line) anchor the Ward's dream; the Menders (the church-guild) repair the Caul's fraying; and everyone gives thanks, because outside there is only ash.

### Layer 2 — The Quarantine (mid-story reveal, ~Chapter 4–5)
The Caul was not woven from inside. It was woven from **outside**. The Sundering was not a catastrophe that happened *to* the old world — it was something the old world *stopped*, and the Caul is the containment. The Lumen is not a shelter. It is a cell. The Founders were not saviors; they were the condemned — or the wardens of something condemned. The Menders' sacred work of "repairing" the Caul is, from the outside's perspective, the prisoners maintaining their own bars — and nobody inside remembers why.

### Layer 3 — The Sheltered Monster (true-ending reveal)
The thing the outside world condemned was never the people. It was the **Ward** itself. The Ward was the Sundering: a being that consumed the old world's magic — and, near the end, was wounded, cornered, and about to be destroyed. The Founders, who had every reason to hate it, chose instead to shelter it. They wove the Caul around themselves *and* it, accepting imprisonment to spare a dying monster — because they saw that it was not evil, only starving, and only a child of its kind. Sealed in, fed on nothing, the Ward fell into its long dream — and its overflowing, leaking vitality became the Lumen's magic.

**Magic is what mercy did with a monster.** Every spell ever cast in the Lumen is drawn from the pain of a creature spared. This is the thematic keystone of the whole game: the pacifist route's final choice re-enacts the Founders' choice, and the violence route's ending re-enacts the Sundering — with the player as the new monster the next world will have to decide whether to spare.

### Boxed decision — what is outside NOW
Leading candidate (my recommendation, not yet canon): *the war ended generations ago.* The outside wardens died, moved on, or forgot. The quarantine has been unmanned for a century. **The door was never locked from the outside anymore** — the seal persisted through fear and faith alone. Devastating, thematically perfect (fear outlives its cause), and it leaves the outside world open for post-true-ending content. Alternatives: outside is genuinely dead; outside is thriving and knows exactly what's in here and is still afraid. DECIDE BY: end of Chapter 3 writing.

## The Ward
- A vast, wounded, dreaming being at the deepest point of the Lumen (beneath the Wardrest). Nobody living has seen it. Its **name is lost** — deliberately erased by the Founders, because a name is a handle and they feared what could be done with it. Finding the name is a true-ending questline.
- It does not sleep peacefully. Magic drawn gently (Hush, Vim) sips from its dream. Magic drawn violently (Fervor, Rue misused) *inflames* it — regions of heavy casting have nightmare weather, dream-bleed, and Thinning.
- It is aware of the player from the moment they fall in. The player is the first new thing in its dream in centuries. **It loves them, in the way a dreamer loves a stranger who appears in a recurring dream.** This is why death is not the end: when the player dies, the Ward refuses the dream to end that way and begins the passage again (diegetic save/reset — see 05-combat).
- The Ward never speaks in words until very late. It communicates in dream-sequences between chapters: images, weather, gifts.

## The Caul
- A living membrane, part spellwork, part tissue. It frays microscopically with **every spell cast anywhere in the Lumen** — magic is a civilizational debt, metered and rationed by the state in principle, black-marketed in practice.
- The Menders re-sew it at the **Ashline** (the industrial rim region) where it hangs lowest and thinnest. Mender stitching is the only sanctioned large-scale magic use — spending the Ward to save the Caul, a contradiction the church does not enjoy discussing.
- **Wounds**: rare tears. The player fell in through one. An unhealed wound is the state's worst nightmare, and the player's existence is proof of one — this is why the church hunts the player in Act 1, not villainy.

## The people
- **Casters:** everyone born under the Caul has a **thread** — an umbilical connection to the Ward's dream. Thread strength varies wildly. Casting means pulling on your thread while holding the right emotional stance (see 02-magic-system).
- **The Thin:** cast too much and the Ward keeps a piece of you. First your colors, then your memories, then you. The Thin are translucent, desaturated, gentle, and slowly forgetting themselves. They are cared for in hospices like the Vigil (Chapter 1) until they slip fully into the dream. Whether slipping is death, or something stranger, is an open question in-world — the Rue school insists the Thin can be visited in dreams.
- **The Threadless:** born with no thread at all — can't cast, can't be metered, can't go Thin. A quiet underclass ("dims," slur). HIDDEN TRUTH (Chapter 5+): the Threadless are the only people who can touch the Caul without fraying it. They were the Founders' failsafe — the intended menders — and the church built on top of their purpose without knowing it. Fenn's entire arc.
- **The player:** not caul-born. No thread. Casts anyway — because the Ward reaches for them directly, without the umbilical. Everyone who understands threads finds this somewhere between miraculous and obscene.

## Power structures
- **The Warden Regnant** (crown): the royal line carries the master-thread. Each Warden slowly becomes the anchor of the Ward's dream, going Thin over decades on the throne. The current king, **Ossian**, has ruled for sixty Vigils and is almost entirely gone — the palace rations his remaining lucid hours and stages his public appearances. The Lumen is functionally governed by:
- **The Mendery** (church-guild): keepers of the Caul, meterers of magic, holders of the Refuge Myth. Not villains — genuinely holding the world together — but institutionally incapable of surviving the truth. Their inquisitorial arm, the **Cantors**, sing their spells.
- **The Lamplight Charter** (civic/entertainment): keeps the cities bright and morale up. Publicly beloved, privately monstrous (see the Lamplighter, 03-cast).
- **The Archive**: one building, technically illegal, tolerated because the crown needs someone who remembers what the church chooses to forget.

## The Guest (the previous faller)
Centuries ago, one other outsider fell through a wound. They never left. The official record says they died; the truth is they went looking for the Ward, reached its dream, and were **kept** — absorbed, aware, unable to die, folded into the dream that loops the world. The Guest speaks to the player at candles (save points), because candles are where the dream runs shallow. Friendly, funny, starving for company, and profoundly not to be trusted: the Guest has had centuries to learn the dream's mechanics (they are aware of the player's deaths and loops), and they want **out** — and the player is the first door that has ever appeared. Their true name and their era are a Chapter 6 excavation. Flowey-slot narrative function, original flavor: not malice — desperation with perfect patience.

## Naming & language conventions (for continuity)
- Time: brights/dims (days), Vigils (years). "Sixty Vigils" ≈ sixty years.
- Oaths and idioms: "by the stitch," "under the Lid" (colloquial/irreverent for the Caul), "gone Thin," "thick-threaded" (talented caster), "dim" (slur for Threadless — characters we like don't use it, or stop).
- The dead "gutter" (like candles). Funerals are called **snuffings** and are, against all odds, warm occasions.
- No character may reference sun, stars, moon, weather-from-above except in Founders-era texts, dream sequences, or as scripture. This is a hard continuity rule.
