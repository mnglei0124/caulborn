# Regions & Overworld — The Lumen

Seven regions, one per chapter/arc. Story order is fixed (linear spine, Undertale-style) but each region has optional depth that pays off in later chapters and gates the true ending. Structure note: the player falls in at the rim (Fallows) and the story spirals inward and *downward* to the Wardrest — descent as narrative geometry.

Overworld play: top-down, puzzle-forward. Each school learned grants an overworld verb (Hush: still a hazard; Wit: unravel a lock; Vim: grow a path; Rue: hear an echo-clue; Fervor: light/burn). Regions are designed so Chapter-1-only verbs suffice for the spine, and revisits with later verbs open the optional depth. Fast-travel: candle-to-candle, late-game, framed as walking the dream's shallows (the Guest loves this; that should worry the player).

## 1. The Fallows — VERTICAL SLICE
Dim farmland at the Lumen's rim, under the Caul's lowest, dimmest stretch. Root-crops, lantern orchards, the wound the player fell through (sealed behind Mendery tape and one bored guard within the first hour — the door home, bureaucratized).
- **Anchor:** the Vigil hospice. Matron Bell, the three named Thin residents, Fenn underfoot.
- **Tone job:** teach the world's warmth so the darkness has something to cut. The Fallows is poor, kind, and quietly dying — the young leave for Wicktown, the Thin arrive from everywhere.
- **Mechanics taught:** encounters, Reach/Still, first wrought weapon, Breath, first school (Hush, from Bell's fight), candles + the Guest's first conversation, ash and its first refusal (or not).
- **Boss:** Matron Bell. **Exit:** the Lampway, a lit road to Wicktown — leaving the Fallows is walking toward the light, which the game will spend five chapters complicating.

## 2. Wicktown & the Lampfields
The Lumen's bright heart — the Caul glows strongest here, and the Lamplight Charter amplifies it into something almost like day. Markets, bright-festivals, propaganda murals of the Founders. The first place the player sees wealth, and the first place ash is a commodity.
- **Anchor:** the Lamplighter's theater-civic-temple. Cantrix Veil's hunt begins here in earnest.
- **Arc:** Chapter 2 = spectacle hiding appetite (Lamplighter exposed or joined). Veil chase set-pieces run through chapters 2–3.
- **Optional depth:** the Charter's hospice ledgers (Lamplighter evidence); Fenn's estranged mother (one scene, devastating, cut-from-marble).

## 3. The Sedge
Marshland where the dream runs shallow and the Thin drift when hospices can't hold them — a region that is functionally a haunting. Rue heartland. Echoes replay old-world fragments the Mendery would burn if it could reach them.
- **Anchor:** the Archive, on stilts at the marsh's edge. Curator Sallow.
- **Arc:** Chapter 3 = the player learns Layer 2 exists (not its content — its existence: the Founders' texts contradict the hymnal). Veil's faith starts cracking here. Rue school acquired.
- **Encounters:** the Sedge's Thin are the game's saddest fights — Stilling them means helping them finish a memory. Violence here is unusually profitable and unusually damning (Sallow's warning: "Grief is dense. It keeps.").

## 4. The Verdance
The Lumen's larder: cathedral-scale flesh-gardens and grain-vaults, Vim-grown, feeding a sealed nation. Lush, warm, wrong in ways the player can't name at first. Grower Ossa presides.
- **Arc:** Chapter 4 = where the food comes from; the cost of comfort at scale (companion theme to the Lamplighter, subsistence edition). Vim acquired. The quarantine reveal (Layer 2 in full) lands at chapter's end, in the oldest vault, in writing too old to have been edited.
- **Design note:** the region most likely to drift body-horror; hold the tone line — *wrong*, not gross. The Verdance is loving. That's the problem.

## 5. The Ashline
The industrial rim opposite the Fallows, where the Caul hangs low, thin, and visibly frayed — the sky has stitches here. Forges, wright-halls, and the Mendery's fortress-monastery. Fervor heartland; Cantor hymns around the clock, because here they're not ceremony, they're load-bearing.
- **Arc:** Chapter 5 = the Mendery from inside — the church as exhausted maintenance crew, post-reveal. Veil's crisis fight (hymn collapse) is this chapter's centerpiece. Fervor acquired. Fenn touches the Caul barehanded in front of witnesses; everything about how people see the Threadless detonates.
- **Optional depth:** the stitching itself — the player can help mend, the game's only sanctioned Fray-scale casting, and it *hurts the Ward audibly*. Doing it anyway, knowing that, is the chapter's thesis question.

## 6. The Quiet
Hush heartland: the deep ring around the Wardrest where sound thins and then stops. Pilgrim country — the devout walk it silently; text itself sparses out (dialogue boxes shrink; the UI participates). The Guest's voice at candles is the only voice that stays full-sized here, which lands exactly as wrong as it should.
- **Arc:** Chapter 6 = the Guest's era excavated (the second faller's sealed report; what the Ward keeps, and why loops happen); Wit formalized if the player has been learning it; the name-quest converges. The region asks the player to be quiet long enough to hear what the world has been saying under everything — the Ward, dreaming, very close now.
- **Encounters:** near-silent pattern design — telegraphs are visual/tactile only. Hardest design chapter, biggest payoff.

## 7. The Wardrest
The capital-palace over the descent. Marble, archives, the guttering throne, sixty Vigils of managed decline dressed as ceremony. Then: the descent itself, and the Ward.
- **Arc:** Chapter 7 = Ossian; the unthreading; Layer 3; the choice. All routes converge here and then split hard (see 05-combat, endings matrix).
- **The Ward's chamber:** designed last, once everything it must pay off is locked.

## Map logic (for the overworld artist, eventually)
Ring-shaped country: rim regions (1 Fallows, 5 Ashline) opposite each other on the outer ring; 2–4 on the middle ring; 6 an inner ring of silence; 7 the center, lowest point. The Caul is visible from everywhere — the ceiling of every skybox, closer at the rims. A player who checks the map should be able to *feel* the world as the inside of something.
