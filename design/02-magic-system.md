# Magic System — The Gift

## Core premise
All magic in the Lumen is drawn from the dreaming Ward. In-world it is called **the Gift** — an irony the player learns to hear: it was never given. Casting means pulling vitality through your **thread** (your inborn connection to the Ward's dream) while holding a specific **emotional stance**. Magic is therefore never neutral: *how you feel is what you cast.*

## The five schools
Each school is a stance — a manner of touching the Ward's dream. Schools are not classes; anyone can learn any school they can genuinely *feel*. Most people have one native stance and one learned one. Masters of three are rare. Nobody in living memory has held all five sincerely — that's the true-ending requirement.

| School | Stance | Domain | Draws on the Ward's… | Bullet-hell grammar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hush** | Stillness, patience, acceptance | Wards, shields, silence, slowing, anchoring the Thin | …longing for rest | Slow, dense, geometric lattices; safe zones that demand patience and reading, not reflexes |
| **Fervor** | Passion, conviction, anger | Fire, light, force, speed | …fever and fury | Fast aimed shots, bursts, beams; rhythm-synced when sung (Cantors); rewards reflexes |
| **Rue** | Grief, memory, longing | Echoes, memory-craft, speaking with the Thin, illusion | …grief for the old world | Homing "memories"; bullets that replay the player's own past movement path; haunting delay mechanics |
| **Vim** | Yearning, hope, hunger-to-grow | Healing, growth, flesh- and plant-shaping | …will to live | Spreading vines/blooms, zone denial, hazards that persist and grow between turns |
| **Wit** | Guile, doubt, play | Unmaking, counterspell, translocation, luck-bending | …dreams' illogic | Rule-breaking: inverted controls, feints, patterns that lie, fake UI. Historically purged; feared |

**Stance requirement is a story engine.** You cannot cast Hush while furious. A boss whose calm breaks mid-fight *loses access to their own school* and cracks into another — their pattern language shifts live with their emotional state. A gentle character pushed to grief starts casting Rue without meaning to. Combat doc (05) formalizes this as pattern phases.

## Costs — the three-tier debt
1. **Breath (personal, immediate):** casting spends Breath, the body's capacity to channel. Regenerates. This is the mana bar.
2. **The Fade (personal, cumulative):** heavy casting lets the dream keep pieces of you — colors first, then memories, then self. Going **Thin** is magic's true price and the game's central horror. Mechanically: a hidden lifetime-casting counter for NPCs (fixed per character, authored), and for the player a visible consequence only on specific story-scale casts (never a grind tax — Thinning is dramatic, not incremental busywork).
3. **The Fray (civilizational):** every cast frays the Caul microscopically. Individually negligible, collectively existential. The Mendery meters legal casting; a black market ("loose thread") thrives. The player's casting contributes — a fact the game states once, quietly, and never turns into a mechanic until the endgame makes you look at it.

## Who casts, and how the player is different
- **Caul-born:** everyone has a thread; thickness varies. Thick-threaded children are recruited by the Mendery young.
- **Threadless:** no thread, no casting, no Fade — and (hidden lore) no Fray: they can touch the Caul barehanded. See Fenn, 03-cast.
- **The player:** threadless AND an outsider — yet casts, because the Ward reaches for them *directly*, no umbilical. Consequences:
  - The player's casting doesn't Fray the Caul (revealed late; the Mendery's meters read them as null — an early clue hidden in plain sight).
  - The player's stances are *taught by relationships*, not birth. Every school the player learns is learned from a person (or taken from one — see below).
  - The Ward feels everything the player casts, unbuffered. Gentle play soothes its dream; predatory play inflames it. The world's ambient state (weather, NPC moods, the Guest's tone) tracks this.

## Player acquisition & upgrade paths
Two mutually exclusive economies per school — this is the mechanical heart of the morality system:

### Gifted magic (Warmth path)
- Learned at story beats from each school's living master, and deepened through **dream-gifts** — between-chapter sequences where the Ward hands the player something it can't say in words.
- Upgrades come from *relationship milestones* (complete a master's personal arc, resolve their boss encounter mercifully) — mechanically: new spell verbs, cheaper Breath costs, wider timing windows.
- Gifted spells are cooperative in flavor: shields that cover NPCs, calming pulses, shared sight.

### Taken magic (Weight path)
- When a caster is killed, they gutter and leave **ash** — their residue of thread and stance. The player can consume ash to *take* the school directly.
- Taken spells are stronger, cost less Breath, and unlock immediately — no relationship required. They are also *inflamed*: visually corrupted versions, and every use darkens the Ward's dream of you.
- **Killing and consuming are separate choices.** You can kill in self-defense and walk away from the ash. Consuming is always deliberate, always a button you chose to press. Weight comes mostly from consumption, not the kill itself — the game's morality distinguishes desperation from appetite.

### The five masters (acquisition map)
| School | Master | Where | Gifted how | Taken how |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hush | Matron Bell | Ch.1 Fallows | Survive her boss-fight without striking; she teaches you the stillness she fought you with | Kill her; her ash is the first the game ever offers you, over her still-warm bell |
| Fervor | Cantrix Veil | Ch.2–5 (recurring) | Earn her doubt, then her faith; she teaches conviction as care | Kill her mid-crusade; inflamed Fervor is the violence route's workhorse |
| Rue | Curator Sallow | Ch.3 Sedge | Help them re-jar their own slipping memories; grief held gently | Kill a half-Thin archivist and take centuries of grief at once (heaviest single Weight gain in the game) |
| Vim | The Verdance's master — **Grower Ossa** (sketch, Ch.4) | Ch.4 | Tend the flesh-gardens' secret with them | Take it; the gardens react |
| Wit | The Guest (!!) | Ch.6 / candles throughout | The only master who teaches for free, from the first candle onward — because Wit is the school of the desperate, and the Guest is grooming you to be their door | Cannot be taken. The Guest cannot gutter. This asymmetry is a clue |

## Hard rules (continuity-critical — never violate)
1. No magic without stance. Emotionless casting is impossible; feigned emotion produces failed or twisted casts (Wit is the exception that *weaponizes* feigning — that's why it was purged).
2. No magic from nothing. Every cast draws on the Ward. If we ever write a spell with no source, it's a continuity bug or a deliberate mystery flag.
3. Wards/shields (Hush) can stop magic, not matter. Knives still work. This keeps mundane violence dangerous forever and the Threadless relevant in fights.
4. Healing (Vim) accelerates the body's own growing — it cannot restore what the dream has kept. **Nothing heals Thin.** (True-ending question: is rule 4 breakable, and at what price? The name quest says maybe.)
5. Casters sense each other's *stance*, not thoughts. Empathy-radar, not telepathy.
6. The dead do not come back. Rue speaks to echoes and to the Thin in dreams; it does not resurrect. (The player's death-loops are the Ward refusing the dream's end — unique to the player, and the Guest.)

## Magic in daily life (texture for writing)
Metered household charms (a hearth that Hushes nightmares, Vim-grown crops in the Verdance); wrought items — spells forged into tools by licensed wrights, usable by anyone including the Threadless (this is how Fenn fights, and how the player fights before learning schools); black-market "loose thread" casting dens; the Cantors' public hymns that are also, quietly, crowd-calming spells. Every convenience is debt. Nobody says it at dinner.
